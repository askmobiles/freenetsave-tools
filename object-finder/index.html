    How to use it

    1. Tap Take or choose a photo. On a phone you can use the camera.
    2. Wait a few seconds. Each thing the AI finds gets a coloured box and a name.
    3. Tap a name to hear it and to show only that thing. Tap Read all names to hear them all.
    4. Move How sure must the AI be? lower to find more things, or higher to show only sure ones.

    Ideas for learning

    • Word hunt: take a photo of a room and learn the English names of everything in it.
    • Counting: how many chairs, cups or people did it find? Was it right?
    • Guess first: say what you think is in the photo, then check with the AI.
    • Is the AI right? Talk about mistakes. AI can be wrong, which is a good lesson too.

    Which devices work?

    • Computers with a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari work best.
    • Phones and tablets: newer iPhones and Android phones work. Use Quick on older phones.
    • A photo takes about 1–10 seconds after the first download.

    Common questions

    Is my photo uploaded?

    No. The AI runs inside your browser. Your photo never leaves your device and we do not save anything.

    What can it find?

    80 kinds of everyday things: people, animals (dog, cat, bird, horse, cow, sheep, elephant, bear, zebra, giraffe), food (banana, apple, orange, broccoli, carrot, pizza, cake…), vehicles, furniture, kitchen items, sports balls and more. It cannot name things outside this list.

    Can this harm my device or let someone hack it?

    No. It runs inside the browser's safety box like any web page and can only see the one photo you pick. The program files come from this website and from jsDelivr, locked to exact versions. The AI model comes from Hugging Face, locked to one exact version. At worst your device gets warm or slow for a moment. Closing the tab stops everything.

    Which AI does it use?

    Quick: YOLOS-tiny. More accurate: DETR (ResNet-50). Both are open-source object finders (Apache 2.0 licence) that run with Transformers.js on your device.
