About this page

Rokuyō is a cycle of six day-names printed on many Japanese calendars. People check it when choosing a day for a wedding, a funeral, moving house or opening a business. Taian is the luckiest day and Butsumetsu the unluckiest. The cycle follows the old lunar calendar, which is why it sometimes skips or repeats at the start of a lunar month.

Dates are calculated on your device. Please check with your temple, mosque, synagogue, church or a local calendar for religious dates.

Questions

Which Rokuyō day is best for a wedding?

Taian is the most popular. Butsumetsu is usually avoided.

Why are funerals avoided on Tomobiki?

The name is read as "pulling friends along", so many crematoriums in Japan close on that day.

How is Rokuyō worked out?

Add the old lunar month and day together and divide by six. The remainder gives the day name.
