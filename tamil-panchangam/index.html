The yearly thithi is found the Tamil way: the same Tamil month and the same thithi, on the day that thithi covers the afternoon. It uses the method (Thirukanitham or Vakyam) and the place chosen on this page. Without a time, the thithi at sunrise on the day of passing is used. Families and temples follow slightly different rules, so please confirm the date with your priest.

About this page

A panchangam has five parts: the day of the week, tithi (moon day), nakshatra (star), yogam and karanam. This page works them out for any date and for your city, by two methods. Thirukanitham uses the real positions of the Sun and Moon. Vakyam uses the old Surya Siddhanta method that printed Vakyam panchangams come from, so its end times are close to a printed book but can differ, sometimes by a few hours.

பஞ்சாங்கம் என்பது வாரம், திதி, நட்சத்திரம், யோகம், கரணம் ஆகிய ஐந்து அங்கங்கள். முக்கிய நாட்களுக்கு உங்கள் கோயில் அல்லது உள்ளூர் பஞ்சாங்கத்தைப் பாருங்கள்.

Questions

What is the difference between Thirukanitham and Vakyam?

Thirukanitham is calculated from the true positions of the Sun and Moon. Vakyam follows older traditional rules. The Tamil date is usually the same, but tithi and nakshatra end times can differ.

How is Rahu kalam worked out?

The time from sunrise to sunset is cut into eight equal parts. Rahu kalam is a fixed part for each day of the week, so it changes with your city and the season.

Why does the Tamil date change with the place?

The Tamil date follows the rule used in Tamil Nadu. The clock times, such as sunrise and Rahu kalam, are shown for the city you pick.
