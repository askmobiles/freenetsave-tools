How the gas trip calculator works

Fuel used = distance × fuel use ÷ 100. Fuel cost = fuel used × the price per litre. Tick Round trip to double the distance, and enter how many people are sharing to see each person's share.

Example: Toronto to Montreal is about 540 km. A car that uses 8 L per 100 km needs about 43 litres. At $1.50 a litre, the trip costs about $65 in gas (43.2 × $1.50 = $64.80), or $32.40 each for two people.

What fuel use should I enter?

Your car's fuel use (also called fuel consumption) is in the owner's manual or on your dashboard trip screen. As a rough guide: a small car uses about 6 to 7 L per 100 km on the highway, a mid-size car or SUV about 8 to 10, and a pickup truck or large SUV about 11 to 14. City driving uses more than highway driving.

Common questions

Can I use miles per gallon (mpg)?

Yes. Choose Miles and US gallons at the top, then enter the distance in miles and your car's mpg.

Where do I find today's gas price?

Use the price on the pump or a gas price app for your area, and type it in. Prices change every day, so the calculator does not guess them for you.

Is this a fuel estimator for long road trips?

Yes. Enter the whole distance of the trip. For a trip with stops, add up each part first, or use the round trip box for a there-and-back drive.
