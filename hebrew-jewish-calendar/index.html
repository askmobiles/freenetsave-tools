About this page

The Hebrew calendar follows the Moon for its months and the Sun for its years. A leap year adds a whole extra month (Adar I), seven times in every 19 years. The Jewish day begins at sunset, so every holiday starts on the evening before the date shown.

Dates are calculated on your device. Please check with your temple, mosque, synagogue, church or a local calendar for religious dates.

Questions

When does a Jewish holiday begin?

At sunset on the evening before the date shown here.

When is Shabbat candle lighting?

18 minutes before sunset on Friday in most communities. The time for your city is shown on this page.

How many months are in a Hebrew year?

Twelve in an ordinary year and thirteen in a leap year.

What year is it in the Hebrew calendar?

The year is shown in the details. It changes at Rosh Hashanah, in September or October.
