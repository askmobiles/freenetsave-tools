About this page

The Islamic calendar is purely lunar: 12 months of 29 or 30 days, about 11 days shorter than the solar year. This page uses the Umm al-Qura calendar of Saudi Arabia. Many countries and communities wait to see the new crescent moon, so the real date where you live can be one day different.

Dates are calculated on your device. Please check with your temple, mosque, synagogue, church or a local calendar for religious dates.

Questions

Why can the date differ by a day?

A month begins when the new crescent is seen, and that depends on the place and the sky. Calculated calendars and local sighting can differ by a day.

When does Ramadan start?

On the first day of the ninth month. The next date is shown in the details, by the Umm al-Qura calendar.

How are the prayer times worked out?

From the position of the Sun for the place you choose. You can pick the calculation method and the Asr rule. Your mosque may add a few minutes, so follow its timetable.

When does the Islamic day begin?

At sunset. The date shown here is the Hijri date for the daytime of the English date.
